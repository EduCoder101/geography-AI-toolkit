After you get the output

            
    
                
  • Use the vulnerability analysis as a starting point for design decisions, not a report card. The question is not "is my task bad?" but "where do I want to focus my effort?"
    • 
                
  • Include marking criteria if you have them. The AI will check whether your rubric would catch geography slop.
    • 
                
  • Follow up: "I can add one lesson for an in-class component. What gives me the most protection?" or "Which speed bumps also improve the geographical thinking, regardless of AI?"

Differentiate a geography resource

                Use when you need to adapt a resource for different learners
            

        

        
Differentiation needs more nuance than a single prompt can provide. The Prompt Builder walks you through a series of questions about your resource, your students, and the specific adjustments you need, then assembles a tailored prompt. It handles support, extension, EAL/D, and neurodiversity adjustments, and lets you choose between a single prompt or a chain prompting sequence.


        
            Open the Differentiation Prompt Builder
            
        

        

            
What the Prompt Builder does differently

            
    
                
  • It asks you who you are differentiating for (support, extension, or both) and what specific adjustments you need (readability, vocabulary, cognitive demand, EAL/D scaffolding, neurodiversity accommodations).
    • 
                
  • It assembles a prompt that targets exactly what you asked for, with your resource and context already embedded.
    • 
                
  • It offers a choice between a single master prompt or a chain prompting sequence that tackles each adjustment type separately.
    • 
                
  • It includes a response checklist so you know what to verify before using the AI output.
