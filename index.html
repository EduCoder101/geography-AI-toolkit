What is this toolkit?

Geography-specific AI resources, not generic education prompts

This toolkit was built for geography teachers who want to integrate AI into their practice without losing the discipline. Every resource on this site is grounded in geographical thinking: spatial reasoning, place-based analysis, scale, evidence-based argument, and inquiry.

Everything here is portable: it works with any AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, or anything else), in any school, with no special software or access required.

Paste a prompt. Get a result. Use your professional judgement to refine it. That is the entire workflow.

The toolkit

Understand how AI works

Before you use AI in your classroom, understand what it actually does. LLMs are prediction engines, not knowledge engines. This page explains why that distinction matters for geography, what "geography slop" looks like, and how the cognitive co-worker framing changes the conversation.

Use when: you want the foundations

Prompt AI more effectively

Stop getting generic output. 28 geography-specific strategies show you the vague prompt you would instinctively type alongside the version that produces useful, grounded results. Filter by fieldwork, case study, assessment, or resource creation.

Use when: you're prompting AI for geography resources

Guide students' AI use

Paste a task description, get a structured table showing students how to use AI purposefully at each stage of their work. Includes the contrast between productive prompting and cognitive off-loading, so students learn to see the difference themselves.

Use when: you're issuing a task where AI is permitted

Audit a task for AI vulnerability

Before you issue a task, find out where students could use AI to bypass the geographical thinking. Get an honest assessment: what's a speed bump, what's a structural protection, and what a geography slop submission would actually look like for your specific task.

Use when: you're designing or reviewing an assessment

Design AI-resilient tasks

The permit–curate–validate framework for geography assessment in an AI world. Understand which changes to your task actually protect assessment integrity (structural protections), which just make it better geography (speed bumps), and which make off-loading visible (detection layers).

Use when: you're rethinking how you assess

Differentiate a geography resource

An interactive tool that walks you through questions about your resource, your students, and the adjustments you need. It assembles a tailored differentiation prompt covering support, extension, EAL/D, and neurodiversity. Foundation scaffolds without removing the geography. Extension deepens without adding volume.

Use when: you need to adapt a resource for different learners

Design principles

Geography first

Every resource targets spatial reasoning, place-based analysis, and geographical inquiry. Not generic education prompts with geography labels.

Tool-agnostic

Works with any AI tool. No accounts, no subscriptions, no specific platform. Paste a prompt and go.

Honest about limits

AI produces geography slop: text that looks like the discipline but doesn't do what the discipline does. This toolkit helps you see it and address it.

The approach

The resources on this site are drawn from work on AI integration in a K–12 school context. The original tools generate structured AI guidance for students across any subject: a teacher enters a task, and the system produces a table of purposeful prompts calibrated to the right level of AI use.

What you see here is the pedagogical concept extracted into portable prompts that any geography teacher can use. The master prompts, the prompt pathway, the task design framework, and the differentiation tools are all adapted for a geography audience and work with any AI tool, in any school.

About

Patrick Ell is a K–12 AI Integration Lead based in Sydney. He works across a school community to support teachers in using AI as a cognitive co-worker: a tool that extends professional capability without replacing professional judgement.
